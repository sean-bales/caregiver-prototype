Caregiver, the family app

A prototype of the app, and why it looks different

I took the family app apart and put it back together around what a family member opens it for. Nothing here is built yet. It is a clickable prototype of every screen and state, on a phone and on a desktop, drawn only from what the app does today. Where a screen shows something the app can't do yet, it is marked "rebuild behavior".

Open the prototype Every screen, listed
Pick a width

The first page asks phone or desktop. Both share the same screens and the same household, so you can switch at any point and land on the same frame.

Tap through it like the app

Tabs, buttons, replies, reminders, and the composer all work. Typing, waiting, and sign-in are stood in for with a single tap.

Use the switcher for the rest

A small control at the edge of every screen changes the situation: device offline, session ended, texts off, a failed save. Every state is three choices away.

What changed

Seven pages became four tabs

Most of the old pages were one job that belonged inside another page. The nav is now the four things a family member actually comes back to.

Alex opens this app between other things, usually for under a minute, to see three things: is the device on, did Daniel ask Stella to pass anything along, and what happened with the medication reminder. The old home page led with memoir progress. The new one leads with those answers, and the actions that follow from them.

Still open

What I decided, and what only you can

I made calls where I had to keep moving. Every one is reversible. The first group needs your answer before the build; the second is what I assumed, and each is labeled "rebuild behavior" in the prototype.

Your call

Questions the code can't answer.

You
Which names a family sees

Caregiver, Legavo, Companion, Conversationalist, and Stella all appear in the app today. The prototype uses Caregiver, Legavo Inc., and Stella, and never names the device. The text-message consent line still says "from Legavo" because that is what was registered with the carrier.

You
What the device does with what we send

The app writes a request and the device picks it up on its own schedule. The prototype never claims the device did something unless the device reported it back. Confirming that reading needs the device code, which we haven't seen.

You
Controls while the device is offline

The server accepts pause and night light while the device is off. The prototype shows them, and shows the request waiting. Whether to offer them at all is your call.

You
Whether texting is live for families

Settings is drawn for both, set up and not set up on the server.

You
What a viewer can do

The viewer role exists in the code with no consistent rule for what it blocks. The prototype shows a read-only Settings screen for it and nothing more.

Assumed

Calls I made so the design could move. Cheap to reverse.

Assumed
One control waits at a time

Pausing voice or switching night light waits on that control only. Today one request locks the whole page.

Assumed
Engagement counts memories, not conversations

The server has no per-day conversation count. The line reads "Stella saved two memories today", which is what the data supports.

Assumed
No "add to home screen" on desktop

The invitation is phone only. Desktop browsers would offer it too; it isn't worth the space there.

Assumed
Deleting a photo says what it deletes

Delete removes every copy, including ones the device added, with no undo. The confirmation says exactly that.

Found on the way

Two defects in the current app, worth an engineer's hour before anything else.

Defect
Password reset fails in production

A date comparison mixes two formats and every reset attempt errors out. The tests pass because they don't hit the real database. One-line fix.

Defect
Inbound texts are accepted unsigned

When the texting token is empty, the app accepts any post as a text from a family member and puts it on the person's screen. It should refuse instead.
